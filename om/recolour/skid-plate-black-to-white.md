# Skid Plate — Black → White (grey hero backdrop)

Recolours an approved black skid-plate photo to neutral white textured powder coat on a **#6B6B6B gradient grey** backdrop with cinematic key/fill/rim lighting.

**Model:** Nano Banana Pro on Higgsfield  
**Upload:** Image 1: the approved black product photograph.

### Before you paste

- This one **deliberately changes lighting and background**, unlike the other recolours, which freeze them.

### What this prompt is guarding against

- White blown out to smooth plastic → exposure ceiling (~80% brightness) + "the plastic test"
- Part looks like it's floating → "the floor area is the darkest part of the backdrop" + dark contact-shadow core
- White silhouette dissolving into the grey → the rim/kicker light is "the most important element"
- Halo around the part → "the gradient belongs to the backdrop, not the product"

## Prompt

**6,529 characters**

```text
Recolour the uploaded product photograph. This is a COLOUR CHANGE ONLY — the same photograph of the same physical part, repainted and re-backed. Not a new shot, not a new angle, not a re-design.

WHITE: every powder-coated surface changes from black to a clean, pure, NEUTRAL white — not cream, ivory, beige, off-white, warm-white, blue-white or grey. That means every panel, fold, facet, side wall, flange, bracket, tab and mount, and every weld bead. The part is welded first and coated afterwards, so the beads are white too — visible as raised rippled texture, never bare metal. Keep the identical TEXTURED powder coat it already has: the same fine sandy micro-grain, the same low gloss, the same soft broad highlights. Only the pigment changes. Do not make it glossy, wet-look, pearl or metallic.

HARDWARE STAYS AS IT IS: every bolt head, nut and washer keeps the exact bright bare-metal finish it already has — polished silver, unchanged. Each fastener is TWO things at one spot from the paint's point of view: the bracket or plate it sits on turns white, the fastener does not. Paint stops dead at the edge of every fastener. Do not paint them white, do not tint them, and do not let the white coating creep onto them. Do not add, remove, move or resize a single fastener.

EXPOSURE — THIS IS THE MOST IMPORTANT SETTING. Expose for the white part, not the background, and expose it CONSERVATIVELY. The single most common failure on this part is over-exposure.

• NO pixel on the part reaches pure white. The brightest white surface in the image sits at roughly 80% brightness, leaving clear headroom at the top. Even the lightest, most directly lit plane is visibly a light grey-white, not paper white.
• THE PLASTIC TEST: the textured powder-coat micro-grain must be clearly visible on EVERY surface, including the brightest upper-facing planes. If any panel looks like smooth plastic, vinyl or moulded polymer, it is over-exposed — bring it down until the grain returns.
• Each folded facet sits at a visibly different brightness from its neighbours, so the geometry reads as distinct planes rather than one bright mass. Shaded sides go meaningfully darker.
• Every edge stays crisp and defined. No clipped highlights, no blown-out flat areas, no glow, halation or bloom, no soft or fuzzy contours. The part reads as clean bright white with full detail and dimension — never as a blown-out highlight. If in doubt, expose darker. Cinematic lighting means MORE tonal range, never more brightness — the drama comes from deeper shadows, not hotter highlights.

LIGHTING — CINEMATIC, DIRECTIONAL, SCULPTED. Relight the part with premium studio lighting that gives it real form and drama. Not flat, not evenly washed, not a spec-sheet lighting setup:

• A large soft KEY LIGHT from the upper front-left, angled about 45 degrees, establishing a clear lit side and a clear shadowed side. Committed direction — the part should obviously be lit from somewhere.
• A gentler FILL from the opposite side at roughly a third of the key's strength, so the shadowed planes stay open and readable with detail in them, but remain unmistakably darker than the lit planes. Never lift the shadows to match the key.
• A crisp RIM / KICKER light from behind and above, skimming the top edges and the far silhouette of the part. This draws a bright defining line along the upper folds and the outer contour so the white separates cleanly from the grey behind it. This edge light is the most important element — without it the white silhouette dissolves into the background.
• Crisp specular lines catch every folded edge and cut edge, defining the geometry. Deep tonal range: the shadowed faces sit meaningfully dark, the lit faces sit bright but never clipped, and each folded facet reads at its own distinct value. Rich, dimensional and premium — while still honest to the product.

BACKGROUND: a neutral grey seamless studio backdrop around #6B6B6B, with no blue, green or warm cast, and no texture, walls, corners, seams, props or visible lighting gear.

Give it a SMOOTH, DELIBERATE GRADIENT rather than a flat field: softly lifted BEHIND and ABOVE the part in the upper middle of the frame, falling off gently darker toward the frame edges and the upper corners. The falloff must be perfectly smooth and continuous — no banding, no blotches, no patchy areas, no hard-edged pool of light, and NO bright halo or glowing ring outlining the part. The gradient belongs to the backdrop as a whole, not to the shape of the product sitting on it.

THE FLOOR AREA IS THE DARKEST PART OF THE BACKDROP. The surface immediately beneath and around the base of the part is noticeably DARKER than the backdrop behind it, and it darkens further as it approaches the part. Never brighten the area under the part — that is what makes a product look like it is floating.

GROUNDING — THE PART MUST SIT ON THE SURFACE, NOT HOVER ABOVE IT. This is critical and it is the second most common failure. At EVERY point where the part physically touches down, render a firm CONTACT SHADOW with a genuinely DARK, TIGHT CORE right at the point of contact, spreading and softening outward from there. The shadow is dark enough to read clearly against the grey and unmistakably anchors the part to the surface. There must be no gap, no glow and no light leak between the metal and the ground at any contact point.

The surface has only the faintest sheen — a very subtle, short, low-contrast reflection directly beneath the part, fading out almost immediately. It must never be mirror-like, never sharp, and never brighter or more prominent than the contact shadow. If the reflection competes with the shadow, reduce the reflection: the shadow is what grounds the part, the reflection is only a hint of surface.

Clean grey shows through every slot, hole and cut-out.

CHANGE NOTHING ABOUT THE PART ITSELF. Keep the exact same camera angle, viewpoint, perspective, framing and crop. Keep every panel, fold, facet, slot, hole, cut-out, bracket and mount exactly as it is. Keep every weld bead exactly where it is, at the same size and texture. Add nothing, remove nothing, reshape nothing. Only the paint colour, the lighting and the background change.

RENDER QUALITY: photoreal, tack sharp, crisp well-defined edges, accurate powder-coat material, rich cinematic tonal contrast with the white holding full detail throughout, neutral true-to-life colour, subtle film grain, gentle highlight rolloff. Premium hero-shot product photography — the kind that sells the part.
```
