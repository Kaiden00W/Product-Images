# Crash Bars — Black → White (match a reference image)

Short prompt: recolour black crash bars to white, matching the finish and **dark charcoal** background of a reference photo.

**Model:** Nano Banana Pro on Higgsfield  
**Upload:** Image 1: the black crash bars, front view (the part and the angle). Image 2: a white crash-bar photo on dark grey (colour, finish and background reference only).

### Before you paste

- This is the shortest OM prompt. It's a good starting point when you have a finished reference to match.

### What this prompt is guarding against

- Camera drifting toward Image 2's angle → "camera — locked to Image 1"
- White blown out → exposure section

---

## Prompt

**2,459 characters**

```text
Two reference images. IMAGE 1 (black crash bars, front view) is the PART and the ANGLE — this is what you are photographing. IMAGE 2 (white crash bars on dark grey) is ONLY the colour, finish, and background reference. Ignore Image 2's camera angle and geometry entirely.

CAMERA — LOCKED TO IMAGE 1: keep the exact same front view, camera angle, viewpoint, perspective, framing, and composition as Image 1. Do NOT rotate, tilt, re-angle, or recompose toward Image 2's angle. Image 1's symmetrical front view is the shot.

GEOMETRY — EXACTLY AS IMAGE 1: preserve every tube, bend, curve, junction, mounting tab, bracket, foot, and the cross brace exactly as Image 1 shows them, same positions and proportions. Add nothing, remove nothing, reshape nothing.

PAINT — CHANGE BLACK TO WHITE: recolour all powder-coated steel from black to a clean bright white powder coat, matching Image 2's finish — fine even textured micro-grain, low gloss, soft broad highlights, no glossy plastic sheen. Structural components (tubes, brackets, tabs, feet, and the clamp collar bodies) are ALL white. Only the threaded fasteners — bolt heads, nuts, washers on the cross-brace clamps — stay polished stainless steel — bright silver metallic with a crisp specular highlight — exactly as Image 1 shows them. Keep that hardware; do not delete it or paint it white.

EXPOSURE — CRITICAL, DO NOT BLOW OUT: expose for the white part, not the background. The white must hold full detail and dimension — no clipped highlights, no blown-out flat white areas, no glow, halation, bloom, or soft blurry edges. Every tube keeps a visible shaded side and a defined outline. Slightly under-expose rather than over-expose. The powder-coat texture stays clearly readable across all surfaces, and edges stay razor sharp.

BACKGROUND — MATCH IMAGE 2: dark charcoal-grey seamless studio backdrop matching Image 2's tone and depth, with the same subtle floor plane and soft grounded contact shadows beneath the part. Even, no banding, no props, no visible lighting gear.

LIGHTING: soft directional studio lighting that sculpts each tube — clean specular ribbon along the tube crowns falling off smoothly to a shaded side, so the white reads strongly three-dimensional against the dark grey. Controlled and crisp, not flat or washed out.

QUALITY: photoreal, tack sharp, high resolution, accurate powder-coat material, deep clean shadows with retained detail, premium catalogue product photography.
```
